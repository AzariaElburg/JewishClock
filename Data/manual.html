1. What the clock shows

A sequence of short messages in the window, over and over:

  • The Hebrew and the Gregorian date.
  • Shabbat: candle lighting on Friday, Havdalah on Saturday night, the weekly parasha, Shabbat Mevarchim and the special Shabbatot.
  • Holidays, Rosh Chodesh, fasts with their start and end times, the Omer count.
  • Daf Yomi and Mishna Yomit.
  • The rain/dew changes in prayer (see below).
  • The family's events - birthdays, anniversaries, yahrzeits - with the age or number of years.

When the day changes. As in printed Jewish calendars, the Hebrew date - and everything that follows it, Chanukah candles included - changes at צאת הכוכבים, not at midnight or at sunset. Gregorian dates change at midnight.

Rain and dew in prayer. Two separate reminders:

  • משיב הרוח / מוריד הטל - from Musaf of 22 Tishrei and of 15 Nisan. On the day it says "ממוסף", the days before "ממחר".
  • The request ותן טל ומטר / ותן ברכה - from the first weekday Maariv: 7 Cheshvan in Israel, 4 or 5 December abroad; after Pesach from Maariv when Yom Tov ends ("מהערב").

After a switch the clock shows only the words themselves. Nusach Ashkenaz or Sefard and how many days around the switch are shown: Calendar Content. Israel or abroad follows the clock's place.

Events on a Hebrew date show from the evening before (when the Hebrew date begins), or from midnight if you choose so (Calendar Content, Birthdays & Events). An event with both a Hebrew and a Gregorian date shows on each of them; a small blue Star of David after the age means it is the Hebrew date's turn (no star: the Gregorian date's). The age is counted in that calendar.

2. Reaching the clock's page

Everything is set from a phone or computer, in its web browser - nothing to install.

Through the clock's own Wi-Fi (always works)

  1. In the Wi-Fi list choose the clock's own network - it has the clock's name, e.g. JewishClock (password: on the printed start guide).
  2. In the browser open 192.168.4.1.
  3. When done, switch back to your usual Wi-Fi.

Phones and computers sometimes leave a Wi-Fi without internet and go back to the home Wi-Fi by themselves - if the page stops answering, choose the clock's Wi-Fi again. After the clock restarts (an update, an import) its Wi-Fi is gone for a minute; the page tells you to choose it again.

Through the home network

On many home networks the page is also at jewishclock.local (the clock's name, without spaces) or at the clock's address on that network. Some routers don't let devices reach each other - then use the clock's own Wi-Fi.

First start, or after a factory reset

The screen scrolls the setup Wi-Fi's name and password. Join it, open 192.168.4.1, pick the home Wi-Fi from the list, type its password, Connect. To put back a backup instead, choose Restore from backup there (see Backup).

When the home Wi-Fi is gone

Router off, out of reach, or its password changed: the clock keeps its own Wi-Fi, and the screen says which one to join. The page works there as always (192.168.4.1) - e.g. to type the new password in Settings → Wi-Fi. Every two minutes the clock tries the home Wi-Fi again by itself.

3. The Calendar and your events

Calendar shows a month: ‹ › move between months, Current Month jumps back, Greg / Heb choose Gregorian or Hebrew months. The search box finds events by name. The list beside it shows the month's events.

Adding or changing an event

  1. ＋ (new) - or click an existing event to change it.
  2. Name, and the type: Birthday, Wedding, Yahrzeit, Other.
  3. The date: Gregorian, Hebrew, or both. For a Hebrew date tick After sunset if it happened after sunset - the Hebrew date is then the next day's. Adar II for a leap year's second Adar.
  4. Remind ... days before: also show it that many days ahead ("in 2 days").
  5. An icon or a picture (next section).
  6. Save. Delete removes it.

Many events at once: edit them in Excel - see Export & import.

4. Icons and pictures

Each event can carry an icon (cake, candle, rings, ...) or a picture of your own: a photo, or one frame of a video (move the video's slider to the moment). The slider under the picture zooms in and out, and dragging the picture chooses the part that shows; the picture is shrunk to the clock's window. ✎ replaces it, ✕ removes it (the icon is back). Pictures are kept in one library on the clock (Library) - one picture can serve several events.

The clock's own pictures - candles, Havdalah, Torah, Mishna, the event icons - can be replaced with your own: Settings → Pictures. Here a short part of a video becomes a small animation (Start here / End here). Each one can be put back to the original. Room for replaced pictures shows how much space is left.

5. Display and brightness

Settings → Display:

  • Adjust to room light on: the clock follows the room - dim in the dark, bright in daylight. Brightness sets the faintest (dark room; Off = no light) and the brightest (light room) level; Room light sets how much light counts as a dark and as a light room. With Learn room light on, the clock learns those two by itself over the days. The thin "Now" marks show the current values. The clock shows the level while you move a handle.
  • Adjust to room light off: one fixed Brightness.
  • Message mode: Alternating shows one message at a time (Dwell time: seconds each); a message longer than the window scrolls through once. Single Scroll: all messages run by in one line.
  • Scroll speed: how fast long messages move.

6. Calendar Content

Settings → Calendar Content lists every kind of message. For each: whether and how often it shows; drag ⠿ to change the order (higher = earlier). Some have their own choices:

Birthdays & EventsA Hebrew-date event starts the evening before, or at midnight.
OmerShort, medium or the full wording of the count.
משיב הרוח · טל ומטרNusach (אשכנז / ספרד) and the days shown before and after a switch day.

Show year adds the year to the date messages.

7. Wi-Fi, place and name

Settings → Wi-Fi:

  • Clock name - also the name of its own Wi-Fi and of name.local.
  • Up to three Wi-Fi networks (home, family, a spare). The clock joins the one it was on last, otherwise the strongest it hears, and moves to a clearly stronger one when the signal stays weak. The green dot marks the one it is on.
  • The ⋯ menu of a network: Connect (move to it now), Change network (pick from the networks the clock hears, or Other… to type a name; Show shows the typed password), Change place, Remove. Then Save Wi-Fi.
  • Place: each network has its own place (city) - for candle lighting, Havdalah, fasts, time zone, Israel or abroad. A new network's place is looked up from the internet and marked detected; check it with Change place. When the clock joins a network, that network's place becomes active.
  • 24-hour clock: times as 18:30 or 6:30.
  • Reset Wi-Fi: forgets all networks - the clock opens its setup Wi-Fi.

8. Backup, import and restore

Settings → Export & import, one Excel file:

  • Events or Both (events and settings, Wi-Fi and pictures included), then Export - keep that file: it is the backup.
  • Import a file exported before (it may be edited in Excel). The page shows what is in it and asks what to import. Events: replaces the event list, with Undo. Settings: replaces all settings (Wi-Fi too) and restarts the clock.
  • Restore a new or reset clock: on its setup page (first start, see section 2) choose Restore from backup and pick the file - events, pictures, settings and Wi-Fi come back.
  • Factory reset... (Advanced) erases everything - offering a backup first.

9. Updates

An update is one file ending in .jcupdate, sent by email. Settings → Clock software → Update the clock → choose the file → Update.

  • The clock restarts once or twice (about 2 minutes). Choose the clock's Wi-Fi again when the page asks, and wait for Done.
  • The new version starts on trial: if it doesn't get on the Wi-Fi within 5 minutes, or stops working, the clock goes back to the version it had, by itself. Settings and events are never touched.
  • The manual and this page come with each update, so they always match the version.
  • If the clock's page ever doesn't work: 192.168.4.1/update-safe (built into the clock) installs an update too.

10. When something is wrong

  • Unplug and plug in again - safe at any time, nothing is lost.
  • Wrong times or holidays: check the place (section 7). Empty or old calendar: the clock may have no internet - it keeps showing what it downloaded last.
  • Clock software → Problem report → Get log saves a text file with the clock's log and settings (no passwords) - email it.

11. Advanced

Settings → Advanced - not needed in daily use:

  • Window calibration (Offset X, Offset Y, Width, Height, Calibrate): where the visible window is on the screen - only after a change to the box. Tear lines moves two thin lines that can appear in scrolling text above and below the letters.
  • Demo: everything the clock can show, once.
  • Debug time: test the clock at another date (Shift by, Set to (Gregorian), Set to (Hebrew), Apply). Always finish with Back to real time.
  • Holiday & times data (from Hebcal): Refresh now downloads it again, Clear cache drops the stored copy. Your own events are not affected.
  • NTP server: where the clock gets its time. Log level: how much the log records.
